## 2023 · Adaptive Phishing Detection: Harnessing the Power of Artificial Intelligence for Enhanced Email Security

*ROCYS, Vol. 5, No. 1 (Spring 2023), pp. 3–9 · DOI [10.54851/v5i1y202301](https://doi.org/10.54851/v5i1y202301)*

Phishing attacks evolve faster than blacklists, heuristics and static content analysis can follow. This article proposes an adaptive detection system that combines machine learning with natural language processing to identify phishing emails in real time, learning continuously from a dataset of phishing and legitimate messages. It also examines how attackers themselves use AI and social-engineering tactics such as personalised content and psychological manipulation, and shows that the adaptive approach achieves a high true-positive rate and a low false-positive rate compared with conventional methods.

**Keywords:** Artificial Intelligence · Phishing Detection · Email Security · Machine Learning · Cybersecurity Adaptive Techniques

## 2022 · PIR – Private Information Retrieval

*ROCYS, Vol. 4, No. 2 (Fall 2022), pp. 65–70 · DOI [10.54851/v4i2y202207](https://doi.org/10.54851/v4i2y202207)*

Private Information Retrieval (PIR) lets a user fetch an item from a database held by a server without the server learning which item was requested. The article motivates the protocol with concrete scenarios – patent searches, pharmaceutical component databases, digital media stores, military map requests – where only PIR protects the intent of the user, then surveys the existing families of PIR approaches, discusses open problems, and outlines a simple implementation and future directions for PIR-based software.

**Keywords:** Cryptography · Database · Private Information Retrieval · Privacy · Data Security · PIR

## 2019 · DoS and DDoS Attacks on IoT Devices

*ROCYS, Vol. 1, No. 2 (Fall 2019), pp. 85–88 · [Article page](https://rocys.ici.ro/fall-2019-no-2-vol-1/dos-and-ddos-attacks-on-iot-devices/)*

An overview of how Denial-of-Service and Distributed Denial-of-Service attacks target Internet of Things devices. The article explains the attack model and the role of botnets built from compromised devices, why cheap hardware and hard-to-update firmware leave IoT products exposed, and how the growing number of connected devices turns them into a major attack vector against critical internet services. It draws on the NTT Security 2017 Global Threat Intelligence Report and ENISA guidance, and closes with practical security recommendations.

**Keywords:** Denial of Service · Distributed Denial of Service · Internet of Things
