## Abstract

The Internet has opened a parallel platform for communications, exchange of information and goods. While the digital dimension may enrich our lives on many different levels, it leaves us vulnerable to new threats. Nowadays, because of insufficient security of IoT (Internet-connected Internet of Things) the number of DoS (Denial-of-Service) or DDoS (Distributed-Denial-of-Service) attacks have grown from 3% to 6% in 2016. Almost 60% came from Asia, EMEA has 21% and the other 19% came from Americas. The main reason that most of the cyberattacks are originated from Asia is that the region has a weak infrastructure used to commit fraudulent acts.

The NTT Security 2017 Global Threat Intelligence Report showed out all these aspects when it was published. At the base of the report were lots of factors and lots of resources. The numbers are impressive: networks containing 10,000 clients across five continents, 3.5 trillion security logs, and 6.2 billion attempted attacks and so on. Many sophisticated sensors kept track of cyberattacks for over six months. The favorite of the attacks was a special model of the video camera (over 66% of attacks).

**Keywords:** Denial of Service, Distributed Denial of Service, Internet of Things

## Main contributions

- Explains the DoS / DDoS attack model and the role of botnets ("zombie armies") built from compromised devices
- Describes what IoT devices are and why cheap hardware, shared components and hard-to-update firmware leave them exposed
- Analyses attack statistics from the NTT Security 2017 Global Threat Intelligence Report
- Summarises ENISA security recommendations for reducing the IoT attack surface
